Website Evaluations

The CRAP principles—Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, and Proximity—offer a practical way to explain how a page’s visual choices guide attention and organize information. Here, I compare one site that uses these principles effectively with one whose deliberately busy presentation makes them harder to follow.

Apple

  • Contrast: Large campaign headlines and prominent product images stand apart from restrained backgrounds, making the main message and call to action easy to notice.
  • Repetition: The page reuses a consistent type style, navigation treatment, and call-to-action appearance across product campaigns, helping the different sections feel like one site.
  • Alignment: Product imagery, headlines, and supporting text follow clear, orderly layouts, creating visual connections between related content.
  • Proximity: Each product’s headline, image, description, and action are grouped together, while generous spacing separates one campaign from the next.

LINGsCARS

  • Contrast: Bright colors and bold graphics create strong contrast, but many elements compete for attention at once, so the most important offer is not always the clearest focal point.
  • Repetition: A playful, energetic tone is repeated, but the mix of visual treatments and promotional elements makes typography and emphasis feel less consistent.
  • Alignment: Individual content blocks can be scanned, but the overall page feels crowded and less orderly than a consistent grid would; this weakens the visual relationships between sections.
  • Proximity: The large amount of tightly packed content makes it harder to tell which details belong together and where one promotion ends and another begins.

The site’s energetic style is distinctive and intentional, but its density makes quick scanning and finding a primary next step more difficult.
